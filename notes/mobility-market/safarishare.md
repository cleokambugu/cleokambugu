# SafariShare (safarishare.com): benchmark

## What it is

Uganda's carpooling platform for commutes and travel, launched around 2020–21 ("Safari Share
app aims to make travel cheaper, safer for Ugandans", Nile Post, August 2021). More than 20,000
downloads on the carpool model between 2020 and 2022. In 2026 it relaunched as a multimodal
marketplace: online bus ticketing across operators, a multi-operator directory, mobile money,
and a bus management system for operators, with a partnership with the Uganda Bus Operators
Association. A native app is expected in Q3 2026; Google Maps Transit integration is planned.

## What the site and app do (as described)

- **Compare across operators before you book.** Check schedules days ahead, compare departure
  times across operators, reserve a seat.
- **Book and pay where the operator allows; otherwise call ahead.** For operators with online
  booking the seat is paid and confirmed immediately. For the rest the schedule and contact
  details are still listed so the rider can phone to confirm.
- **Operator pages.** A directory with a page per operator (Comfort Coaches, Tausi and others),
  tickets and routes.
- **Route pages.** "All available bus and carpool routes"; each listing shows the terminal or
  pickup point with the stop name and, where available, a Google Maps link for directions.
- **Verification.** Every member's account is verified with a phone number and an ID or
  passport.
- **Carpool matching.** Enter location and destination, choose a driver, book a seat; pitched
  as avoiding the trip through the congested city to the park.
- **Agents.** A nationwide network of registered agents who book on behalf of customers and
  earn mobile-money commissions.
- **Roadmap.** Carpooling, special hire, self-drive rentals, and parcel delivery on existing bus
  routes.

## What UG takes from it

| SafariShare pattern | What UG does with it |
| --- | --- |
| Compare departure times across operators on one corridor | The stage board gains a **buses on this corridor** strip: operators, departure times, fare, and the pickup park, next to the stage cards, so a rider sees the bus and the stage side by side. |
| "Call ahead" for operators without online booking | Every operator row carries a state: **Book on UG** where a channel exists, **Call ahead** with the number where it does not. Honest listing beats a dead button. |
| Pickup point with a map link on every route | Stage cards already carry a fixed pickup point; the bus strip adds the park and a map link (Felt when connected, otherwise the plain maps link). |
| Phone plus ID verification | UG's ladder: phone in the Handshake, vouches for visibility, documents for driving. ID capture at the first payment is added to the v2 list. |
| Registered agents on mobile-money commission | UG's **agents**: the stage chairman as a first-class account who signs riders and drivers and earns per booking. The panel's chairman asked for exactly this. |
| Parcels on bus routes (planned) | UG ships it now as **Parcel on the Stage** and lists the bus parcel office beside it. |
| Operator pages and a bus management system for operators | v2: an operator console (seats, schedules, payouts) so buses become inventory the stage board can top up from at cut-off. |

## What UG does differently

SafariShare lists supply and lets riders choose. UG pools paid demand first and manufactures the
car; buses are the fallback and the comparison, not the product. SafariShare sends a rider to
call the operator; UG's concierge places the booking and keeps the rider on the rail.
