The Virtual Stage · seats fill first, then the car comes

Pay for the seat. Watch the stage fill. The car is manufactured when the money is in.

Like a matatu stage, a car leaves when it is full or at the cut-off. Unlike a stage, you queue money from your sofa, the seat price falls as friends join, and the driver who takes it is one who already said they are comfortable on that road. Not full at cut-off, you are refunded in full the same hour, and airport-night stages carry a guarantee: a special hire at a capped price instead of a refund.

Every corridor also has buses. Compare departure times across operators, see the park you leave from, and book on UG where the operator takes online bookings. Where it does not, we say so and give you the number to call ahead, rather than a dead button.

The Atlas · every way to move, one table

Air, bus, water, rail, road, and the car you actually want. Uganda is growing airports, ferries and a railway; UG quotes all of it on one screen.

Complete transport arbitrage means the helicopter and the matatu sit in the same list, priced honestly, with a state that tells the truth: Book on UG where a channel exists, Call ahead where it does not, Listed so an operator is recognised before it is wired, Coming for what is being built. The picky traveller gets the Signature fleet; the weekend gets a stay on the same receipt.

Foresight · demand before it happens

Mango season needs minibuses in Masaka. Entebbe needs cars at midnight. Namboole needs sixty special hires at four. UG says so a week ahead, and counts who promised to be there.

The system behind the app reads three calendars: the harvests by region, the rhythms of airports, hospitals, markets, campuses, churches and stadiums, and the named events of the year. From those it forecasts loads per day by place and vehicle class, shows the gap between what is needed and what drivers have promised, and points the right vehicle to the right place. As stages fill and trips complete, the forecast learns from what actually happened.

Confidence is shown per signal; seasons are rules until there are fills to learn from.

Deliver · send the thing

Cakes to Bugolobi, a passport to Jinja, a sofa to Mukono. Every courier in town quoted, and the boot of every stage car for sale.

The flip side of a ride is a parcel. UG quotes the boda couriers, the local delivery apps where they exist, and its own Parcel on the Stage: a box rides upcountry in the boot of a funded stage car for a quarter of a seat. You book and track here; nobody sends you to another app.

Pool · share the seat, split the fuel

The Friday Jinja run, the Saturday Mbarara dash, the 4 a.m. airport drop. Someone in your circle is already driving.

Verified phone, verified plates, verified employer or church or alumni circle. Riders and drivers rate each other, chat inside the trip, and the fare is the true cost of the road divided by the seats. No surge, no meter. The driver's wage is in the price and shown in shillings, and so is UG's fee.

Offer a seat, or see what a seat should cost

Posted trips live only in this browser until you sign in. Pool follows the same pattern the Pool app popularised: phone-verified accounts, a trip chat, and two-way ratings, plus circles so you ride with people you already half-know.

Partners · we send you demand

Competitors are listed on purpose. A rider who trusts the comparison comes back for the pool and the rental.

UG is a marketplace first. Ride-hailing apps get pre-filled bookings with no acquisition cost. Rental yards get vetted weekend renters. Lodges get groups arriving on a known date. Drivers get a route full before they leave.

For riders

Verified drivers and plates, an in-trip SOS that shares live location with a contact you choose, helmets on every pooled boda, and a receipt for every shilling.
